# Narrative craft for the new format

Use a **single event with escalating consequences**, rather than a television episode touring several species. Let the viewer understand who is vulnerable, what is closing in, where escape might be possible and why each apparent solution fails.

## Opening promise

- 0–6s: visible imminent danger or an unstable position. No scenic warm-up, branding or broad factual lecture.
- 6–12s: show the vulnerable individual and relationship worth following.
- 12–18s: reveal the advancing threat and readable approach direction.
- 18–24s: show how terrain closes an option.
- 24–30s: leave one concrete action unresolved. The final escape, injury or death must not be shown.
- 30–60s: establish the original situation, route and stakes through action. Avoid a second trailer or exposition reset. A brief narrated temporal reset is allowed if the hook borrows a future moment.

This 30+30 shape is the user's production choice. The references do not both use a climax montage at the start. Do not claim the exact layout was measured in both references.

## Middle and payoff

Plan proportional acts after the opening: first pressure/first escape (~20%), compromised safety/new obstacle (~25%), tactical reversal (~25%), decisive attempt (~20%), immediate aftermath (~10%). Adjust to events; never repeat the same attack solely to fill minutes. Longer requested films require more meaningful choices/terrain problems, not slower narration.

Every 20–40s, introduce a new fact with a consequence: a route narrows, a foothold fails, a youngster falls behind, a predator finds an alternate line. This is an editorial guideline, not a proven retention threshold. Let preparation make the eventual solution intelligible: show the ledge/crack before it determines the outcome.

Intercut threat → vulnerable subject → terrain → reaction while maintaining geography. Re-establish the route with a high wide after a substantial position change. Let wide shots explain risk, medium shots show action, close details show the mechanism. With 6–9s holds, vary composition and action within the take; do not force 1s montage edits into this contract.

Keep consequences: a slipped animal remains below; a crossed gap stays crossed; the same mother/young/predator retains identity, injuries and travel direction. Use cuts motivated by gaze, action or sound. A new scene cannot magically restore the old arrangement.

End by paying off the opening question. Give the surviving pair a clear immediate state, allow a short emotional release and finish. Avoid a two-minute unrelated animal/conservation montage in an eight-minute film. Do not villainize a hungry predator or state unsupported universal claims.

## Narration

Use specific physical verbs and connected paragraphs; vary sentence length. Explain the consequence the image cannot fully state. Avoid describing every paw movement or repeating “one wrong step” across beats. Keep factual explanation attached to a visible survival problem.

Use 85–92% planned voice-file occupancy. Leave short, purposeful gaps for an impact or change of direction, not minute-long unvoiced walking/nursing loops. Do not promise any retention percentage without the channel's actual Analytics.
