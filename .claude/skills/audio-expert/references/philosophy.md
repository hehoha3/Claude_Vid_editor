# Philosophy (from audio_expert_plan.md, paraphrased)

Order of thought: story -> viewer emotion and knowledge -> sound map -> assets -> placement -> check -> feedback.

Five principles
1. Emotion before audio - decide what the viewer should feel first.
2. Every sound tells something - an empty track is not a reason to place a sound.
3. Establish -> Break -> Reveal -> Restore - normal state, one element changes, the event, the world returns.
4. Sound before image only with a reason (movement heard before the animal is seen); not a formula.
5. Few sounds, the right ones - one clear detail beats five cinematic layers.

Before each sequence answer: what should they feel / what do they know and not know / which sound should they notice /
what change tells them the story moved on.

Applying them
- Ambience is a living world: keep it consistent, then change it. In V1 thin it by removing layers, not by lowering volume.
- Offscreen sound: passive (stable space) or active (a question about the source). Active ones need grounds in picture/story.
- Gaps tell: not every narrator gap gets music. Some need natural sound, some only forest, some need layers removed.
- Aftermath needs time: after an attack keep breath, leaves, water before ambience and music return.
- Motifs must mean something: note the motif, where it appears and how it changes (this film: M-calm, M-danger, M-drop).
- Nature may continue unchanged after loss; use it when the scene wants contrast.
- No fixed percentages of music or natural sound; decide per sequence.

Foreground: one focus per beat plus the fewest support layers (start with 1 + 1-3). The narrator has priority while speaking;
V1 helps with timing, gaps, layer count and low-competition sources, but cannot guarantee intelligibility without mixing.
