# Storycraft and footage truth

## Narrative DNA

Two formats (user 2026-11-03; ask which if the request does not say):
- **(1) Central question:** a question the viewer answers more deeply after each story. Each sub-story has its own smaller question and a concrete local answer. The ending synthesizes those answers rather than crowning one animal.
- **(2) Multi-story:** 3–5 stories, linked or independent (species, place or theme may change). Each has its own question, climax and ending; no forced synthesis, one closing line ends the film. Order by energy: Story 1 strongest, change mechanism and tempo between the others.

Intro: show a living place before danger, let a line of narration turn ordinary movement into a question, map the danger before a fast shot, and tease only encounters that the film later pays off. The user's preferred default is fifteen gentle seconds of landscape **and** animal routine under soft music (the intro track, low, no drums), then naturally escalating drum-driven action. The last fifteen seconds of a 90-second intro breathe.

Story 1 (both formats; it also feeds the intro teasers): identify an individual or coherent group with a practical goal (food, safe path, offspring, water). Runs 8–15 minutes. Fast action within its first 30 s (a chase, a leap, a scramble), first attack by ~03:00 of the film, then escalate: two or three action passages, each with a reversal (failed approach, dodge, changed footing, rival, return journey), before consequence and local resolution. Pick the species by how much strong motion footage exists for it.

## Motion (user 2026-11-03)

- Intro and Story 1 are built mostly from strong motion shots: chases, sprints, pounces, dodges, leaps, climbs up rock, cliff or tree (leopard, klipspringer, baboon; snow leopard, wolf elsewhere), sparring and fights shown with contact but no wound or blood.
- No slow motion in any shot description: they feed the AI video prompts (missing-prompts), and the user wants no slow-motion generated footage. Write real-time action verbs.
- Animals are always doing something. No shot of animals staring at each other, standing still or posing; a calm passage is still movement (walking, drinking, stalking, playing, climbing) and carries new information.

Other stories: change the mechanism. Examples include early detection vs blind approach, group defense vs isolated speed, water access vs the inability to remain, or feeding now vs caching for later. Change tempo after an action passage with an observation that conveys new information. Keep each story linked to the question, not just to the setting.

Narration: use precise, visual verbs and measured stakes. A short sentence may land after a strike; an explanatory thought may need two or three sentences together. Let sound and image carry actions already obvious on screen. Avoid omniscient intent, repetitive rhetorical questions, generic superlatives and victory-language after mere separation.

## Ecological and editorial gates

- Choose the actual geographical unit. Do not describe Pantanal evidence as an Amazon observation or use a southern African research result as proof of one Serengeti herd. Distinguish context from claim.
- Identify exact species where footage permits. If species, sex, infant relationship or behavior cannot be confirmed, use a broader label and do not invent it.
- A real short contact can be shown briefly. Preserve predator and prey route, count, orientation, action and outcome across cuts. Do not turn independent clips into a continuous kill or rescue. No blood requirement when requested.
- Communication and symbiosis require restraint. A bird calling before monkeys move does not prove the call was directed at the monkeys; an ant-following bird benefits from escaping prey without ants intentionally feeding it.
- Primary research supports general behavior; it does not certify that fictional named individuals or stock clips form the recorded event.
- Prefer medium/wide frames that reveal spatial causality. Use close views sparingly for a meaningful detail. No synthetic impact covering an actual miss.
- Give one animal bible across screenplay and visual plan. Male/female/young locks follow evidence; if unrelated footage replaces a shot, revise all references.
- A footage shortage is an editorial branch: substitute a truthful action and revise voice, translation, screenplay and visual plan together before recording.

## Final review

Watch the first 90 seconds without narration: are the subject, threat and direction clear? Watch Story 1 without music: does it still have a specific question, reversal and consequence? Inspect every sound-only hold: does the viewer learn or feel something new? Scan the shot list for slow motion, still or staring shots and replace them. Format (1): verify the last chapter answers the central question and does not simply repeat the intro; format (2): every story closed its own question.
